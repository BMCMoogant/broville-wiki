Western Desert

RegionsEdit articleSuggest a changeSources

A stretch of sand at the far western end of the continent containing the Egyptian-inspired settlement. The desert also appears in the records of Brian Garlick’s expedition.

The western landscape

On the road map, sand occupies the western end of the continent, beyond the forest belt and the power plant area. The map shows the transition to vegetated land and the shape of the coast. In the isometric view, arms of water separate sandy headlands and small islands near the mainland, and a channel helps distinguish the settlement from the monumental area.

The point named desert in the package list, −3115, 84, 2348, serves as a reference for the Egyptian complex.

The desert in Garlick's account

In Final Notes, Brian Garlick reports that the SOLAR team studied artifacts from desert ruins and interpreted inscriptions as a portal design. After the tests, he began reading the inscriptions as a warning.

The diary gives the desert a role in the history of SOLAR's research, but supplies no coordinates and does not identify the settlement's temple or pyramid as the location of those finds.

Other kinds of remains

The Egyptian complex inventory records named funerary pieces and an item called Sphinx at −3231, 53, 2461. That name identifies the item; the Atlas does not use it as evidence of a structure's dimensions.
