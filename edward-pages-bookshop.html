A bookshop established by Edward Page in a former station in the canals district. Coffee and a railway diorama feature in the bookseller’s account of daily business, while his diary also records noises beneath the floor and a hollow wall behind a bookshelf.

The former station

Bookseller Edward Page converted a deteriorated station into a bookshop in Canals District. In his Journal, he describes the November 10 opening as a success, despite his mother’s concern that a bookshop would have little appeal in the digital age. The year is given as 20XX.

The shop serves coffee, has a children’s section and maintains a train diorama. On November 25, Page reports having to close for a few days to repair the model, which kept getting stuck on its loop.

Noises and research

A few days after opening, Page begins complaining about a noise resembling machinery beneath the floor. The municipal records he consults show only a sewer tunnel a block away; he distrusts the documentation because the station itself was also absent from it.

A newspaper clipping found beneath a floor tile leads him to investigate researcher Dan Brosco. In the files of local newspaper The Broville Beat, Page finds information that the station and warehouse had been part of Brosco’s laboratory and that the researcher had disappeared in the area.

Spoiler warningReveal spoiler

After a customer bumps into a bookshelf, Page notices that the wall behind it is hollow. Opening it reveals an elevator shaft that he estimates to be at least three stories deep. On his descent, he sees two doors blocked by iron gates and the wrecked elevator at the bottom.

Later, old permits and notes from Brosco reveal an intersection between the laboratory tunnels and the municipal sewers. Page manages to open a rusty gate along this route, but postpones entering after seeing security cameras and wanting to explore alone. The investigation continues in The Laboratory Behind the Bookshelf.
