City and continent

Broville is a series of Minecraft city maps started by Oldshoes in 2009 and developed with a team of builders, including contributors from the United States and Canada. Work across multiple versions combines an extensive city, explorable interiors and secrets scattered above and below ground.

v10, released in 2011, focuses exploration on the city and its underground passages. v11, developed in a new world between 2012 and 2016, expands exploration to a continent of snowy mountains, plains, swamps, islands and deserts. Oldshoes associates its landscape and climate with the west coast of North America and cites Victoria as a reference in his 2016 interview.

Terrain and architecture

The terrain in v11 received extensive landscaping, with towering mountains, large open areas and deep caves that give the continent features uncommon in normally generated game worlds. The landscape supports finding hidden places, building, adapting the scenery and establishing a base for Survival play.

Architecture ranges from skyscrapers and commercial areas to residential suburbs. Brompton, Canal Town and Breno are among the names used to introduce the diversity of districts, whose buildings and cultural references change from one area to another. The city also supports free exploration, searches for chests and secret locations, and using the setting for experiments in building or destruction.

Districts and navigation

Downtown contains skyscrapers and BroCORP. The district plan places Canals District south of Uptown and west of Brompton. Farther west, the city gives way to the SOLAR forest and the desert. The snowy mountains occupy part of the south, while Brolapagos lies on an island southwest of the center.

Routes of exploration

  • Between neighborhoods. On the metro, Museum connects the Purple and Green Lines. At Canals East, the Red Line crosses the Blue Line, which serves Brompton.
  • Beyond the center. The regional railway passes through City Port and Braulding and continues to Portvale and Fort Bulwark. The Atlas road map helps navigate between districts, forest and desert.
  • Inside buildings. The Museum of Natural History is a starting point for Chr0NOS. At the asylum, the first BVPS journal is inventoried at X −1320, Y 68, Z 1470. Mission articles identify entrances and place solutions behind a spoiler heading.

These routes overlap with searches for buildings, regions, landmarks, items and documents. A visit can remain within one neighborhood or follow clues through tunnels and remote parts of the continent.

History distributed through the landscape

Underground signs preserve traces of the Black Line. The Egyptian complex in the far west is identifiable on maps through its buildings and in world records through its items. In the center, underground notices and the Doctor's reports offer different perspectives on the sinking. The History of Broville brings these strands together and arranges the dates that can be established from the documents.

Maps included with the download guide exploration; signs, items and books support names, coordinates and episodes. Community descriptions also record local conventions, such as Downtown's classification and the history attributed to the Black Line. A book presents a character's account, while a plan shows spatial relationships for the edition it accompanies. For the relationship between the city released in 2011 and the old city narrated in v11, see From v10 to v11.
