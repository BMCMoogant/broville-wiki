Remains of the network

The Black Line is presented as the first mass transit system of the city in v11. Its remains include tunnels, stations and signs, with very few powered rails. Signs for Mid Side Station are at −328, 66, 1802 and −340, 58, 1805. Another sign at −276, 47, 2139 marks an entrance in the Canals area. A passage sometimes associated with Canopy station is identified on site as an emergency railway depot.

Construction and abandonment

Community history places construction between 1919 and 1923 and attributes most funding to the now-defunct Canopy. Its announced public purpose was to serve the population at large, but exorbitant fares restricted use to the wealthy; the private motives for construction are described as poorly known.

Abandonment is associated with riots across Broville, preceded by the disappearance of numerous members of the elite who were reportedly never heard from again. The chronological relationship between these events and the sinking remains unresolved. The claim that the line's records and diagrams were lost concerns the historical documentation of the old network.

Stations and reuse

The community inventory lists seven stations, three of which remained in use under other networks:

StationDescribed status
Arts District StationAbandoned.
Canopy Corp StationAbandoned.
Library StationAbandoned.
Mid Side StationAbandoned.
Boardwalk StationReused; the v11 Metro Map shows it on the Purple Line.
Brompton StationSurvived the riots and was reportedly adapted for the Blue Line, established in the 1970s.
City Port StationReused by the regional line, described in this account as the Rural Line between Space Station and Portvale.

The v11 Metro Map confirms Boardwalk on the Purple Line and Brompton on the Blue Line. City Port appears on the regional railway diagram. The seven-station inventory belongs to the historical survey of the Black Line and does not replace maps of the later networks.

Inspections in the ruins

An image of an abandoned station is accompanied by a caption presenting a routine police inspection and using the term “euthanasia” of homeless people. The wording reflects the dark humor of the city's description; it does not establish an additional documented episode in the line's history.
