Between the canals and the port

Brompton lies outside the Downtown core, east of Canals District, near Breno and City Port. On the Metro Map, it is the blue line terminus after Stadium. It is a medium-sized neighborhood with inexpensive stores, narrow apartments and many abandoned houses. Its architecture loosely resembles early twentieth-century American buildings.

Expansion and decline

The community history attributes Brompton’s rapid growth to its proximity to the port. A major riot is said to have affected the district, followed by rising crime, abandonment and homelessness. The neighborhood was eventually segregated and walled off, becoming an impoverished version of its former self.

Changes in building style and unusual breaks in the street pattern support the hypothesis that Brompton once included areas now occupied by Breno and CWC Stadium, but insufficient documentation survives to confirm those former boundaries.

Public housing and the Browery

One of the most prominent structures is Broville Great Society Public Housing. Several older buildings were demolished to make room for this large development of inexpensive homes, accompanied by a swimming pool. The first project is described as successful, but funding ran out during construction of the second, leaving a deep excavation.

Over time, tall, cramped residential buildings were erected around a narrow passage at the bottom of the excavation. The area became known as the Browery. The neighborhood’s decline turned it into a dark, run-down area with buildings in poor condition.

Railways and commerce

The Black Line description records the conversion of Brompton’s old station for the blue line. Local commerce also appears in Test Subject Observation Form, found at −1179, 54, 1785: the document states that SOLAR marketing approved the sale of the tested compound through its own ¡El Medico Rapido! stores in Brompton.
