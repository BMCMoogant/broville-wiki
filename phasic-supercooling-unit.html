Development and manufacture

The Phasic Supercooling Unit is equipment intended to cool important heavy-duty machinery. The design originated at SOLAR, but manufacture is attributed to Compass R&D. The community inventory records only two units ever produced, both at the C.E.P. Grid 16 power station.

From prototype to Compass

SOLAR developed the unit to solve a problem with OSHA Hydration units, which failed to maintain a temperature of 31 degrees after being filled with asymmetrical ice polygons. The source does not specify the temperature scale. The first prototype failed: activation flash-froze the entire testing chamber.

After SOLAR went bankrupt, Compass LTD acquired the blueprints and modified the design for other uses. This distinguishes the development's origin from the company responsible for producing the recorded units.

Described operation

The equipment's fictional explanation says it charges its contents with End particles and ejects them into the Void, instantly freezing them. Once the particles are fully discharged, laws whose name is obscured as “#####” force the contents back into the world, completing one phase cycle.

Two units operating in tandem could provide uninterrupted cooling. The stated consumption is 0.00005 ngW to start each cycle and approximately 1.8 ngW/hr per unit; the documentation presents these values without explaining their relationship.
