Excavation beneath the city

The Great Fire of Broville is associated with the construction of the subway tunnels. The community narrative describes blasting caverns large enough to accommodate stations, including the detonation of substantial quantities of TNT beneath heavily populated areas. A miscalculation of the required charge caused what that narrative calls the city's worst disaster in fifty years; no calendar date is supplied, and its position in continuity remains uncertain.

Explosion and spread

At approximately 7:30 a.m., an unknown quantity of TNT exploded beneath Downtown, roughly where Boss Station later stood. The resulting fire reached the wooden foundations of nearby buildings, burned upward through the floors, and destroyed supporting walls.

The Broville Fire Department responded quickly with multiple trucks. The flames nevertheless intensified until the fire was declared out of control.

Thoth17's response

Local fire chief Thoth17 took off from the station roof in the department's only helicopter. Risking his life, he repeatedly dropped loads onto the burning buildings. The account does not specify the material dropped, but says the flames diminished as the burning material was put out and that the fire was under control by 8:00 a.m.

The area was subsequently rebuilt. Thoth17 was rewarded and commemorated in Broville's history books for his response.
