New Archeology Division

OrganizationsEdit articleSuggest a changeSources

The New Archeology Division, or N.A.D., is a Museum of Natural History research hub devoted to exploring, preserving, and revising Broville’s historical records.

Formation and work

The N.A.D. emerged from a merger involving the former Broville Archaeology Division, or B.A.D. Its creation generated tensions in the scientific community amid competing interpretations of Broville's chronology.

As a research hub of the Museum of Natural History, the division explores sites, preserves remains, and updates historical records. It sometimes collaborates with private entities such as SOLAR Corp, Bro Corp, and Compass Ltd, but it is not the only organization studying the city's past.

Field guidance and the destination of finds

Site Objectives, signed by the N.A.D. Office of A.S., requests help gathering a site's artifacts and placing them in the designated container. Books have a specific destination: they must be returned to the Museum of Natural History for immediate examination. The document provides practical guidance for collecting and forwarding finds without naming the location as Site A or B.

Excavations in the Great Plains

Site B

Located west of the Great Plains, Site B studies an ancient local Celtic village. The discovery is considered important because the civilization had not previously appeared in the city's official records.

Site A

In the center of the Great Plains, Site A accounts for a significant share of the division's budget. The hill is surrounded by twelve tapered monoliths. A thirteenth structure appears to emit extraordinary energy, and excavations suggest an underground chamber possibly connected with a figure called The Founder. The emission and this connection are interpretations in the site's description rather than established conclusions.
