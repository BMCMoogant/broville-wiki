The Broville Museum of Natural History brings together paleontology, archaeology, geology, art, and local history in a complex in southern Brolibu. The time-machine mission also begins on its lower levels.

Location and admission

The complex stands in the south of Brolibu District, around −600, 65, 1650; the museum reference point is at −594, 65, 1649. Museum Station provides access from the Purple subway Line and is the Green Line terminus.

The Reception & Admittance desk is near the main entrance. Its advertised fictional fees are:

Permanent collections

Paleontology and primitive humans — levels 0 and 1

Bones McGee is a Steveasaurus-Rex skeleton presented as having been discovered in 2007 near University Square. The collection also includes the Velociraptor Blockasaurus, described as a massive predator that lived alongside the Steveasaurus, and an unknown dinosaur skull whose origins are the subject of academic debate.

The primitive-human section contains artifacts of various kinds. In the museum's characteristic humor, its scientists support the controversial theory that the first humans were all bald and bearded, including the women. Another anecdote accompanies a skull found in a watermelon field: its owner supposedly exploded from eating too much.

Archaeology, the occult, and geology — level 0

The exhibition on the origin of strongholds presents these underground labyrinths as ancient constructions for protection and worship. Its archaeologists interpret the hidden chambers as places where followers were sacrificed to an ancient god. Objects include an unknown, indestructible egg found in a stronghold and a completely undecipherable library.

In the geology section, the Ore Cave reproduces a resource-rich mine.

History and society — level 0

The historical route runs from the V-niner, or v9, era to v10. The v9 presentation jokes about the absence of items that would become everyday necessities, including cauldrons, different types of wood, and carpets. Life Before Disaster recreates a typical v10 residential street before the disaster. The V2 Monument exactly replicates the marker that defined the map boundary in Broville version 2.

Art and shop — level 0

The gallery includes Cavern's Light by Cilliana Brodicci, attributed to the Brovillian Renaissance and saved from the Great Fire by an art thief. Psychology by Enrique Gonzalez is a postmodern work found near the BroCorp Tower.

Near the exit, the shop sells artifact replicas, posters, guides, and toys, including a foam caveman club.

Volcano, depths, and piracy — level 1

A structural replica of a volcano allows visitors to walk through its interior. The underwater section displays ancient squid skeletons and equipment from early submarine exploration. The remains of the South Coast Creature are preserved as a biological mystery.

The pirate wing challenges clichés about local piracy. According to the exhibition, pirates did not use cannons: they preferred boarding other ships by building earth bridges to carry TNT or lava between vessels. The life of Brobeard and the legends of his treasure receive particular attention.

The museum also manages the Warrior Statues, warrior effigies distributed irregularly across the territory, whose relationship to other historical periods remains unknown.

Jungle and desert temples — level −1

The jungle and desert civilizations are represented through ancient trap and puzzle systems, along with a prophetic tablet engraved with hieroglyphs. The tomb of Brotemphotep is the centerpiece. His sacred headdress is presented as evidence that he was killed by an axe blow to the skull.

The Lion Statue was discovered in 1934 in the Great Pyramid of Pharaoh Desomin III. Jungle temples are presented as early technological centers for experiments with redstone and complex traps. A tribal relic found in the 1980s by someone named Billy evokes Indiana Jones-style adventure films.

Private collections and Project Chr0NOS

Staff-only areas on level −1 hold paintings and objects that are not on display and secretly house Project Chr0NOS. The manual is at −594, 37, 1634, below the museum reference point. It requires a fuel core on each side of the engine for every jump and describes the destinations as fragments of space-time with boundaries of their own.
