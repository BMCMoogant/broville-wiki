The library book

A scribe found an ancient tome describing a nearby world, normally imperceptible, and an artifact held by one of its rulers. The Eye of Arburaz supposedly granted strength, vigor, and rejuvenation. Heraldus wanted to put these properties to work for the kingdom with his scholars’ help. He intended to request the object and, if refused, take it by force.

The watchman’s account

A guard saw the air ripple and a dark mass take the shape of a stone building in the swamp. The building was found at the indicated location and matched an illustration in the tome. This coincidence led the king to assemble knights and provisions.

The diaries

The volumes signed H. are a first-person narrative. The king records the division into patrols, defense of the camp, and decisions about wounded companions. His voice dominates the collection, although some events he recounts, such as the first incident involving Robert, come from other knights. The itinerary and final entry are covered in Heraldus’s expedition.

Day 9 Though the night drags on, it will not be long now. The barrage seems to have quieted for the time being, so I shall take a moment to briefly recount what all has transpired since our retrieval of the Eye.

Page 2

Following the battle with the former possessor of the artifact, or "Agraal" as the Eye referred to it as, we sought shelter in a nearby barracks to recover and to prepare for our return to the encampment. On the way there, we were

Page 3

pursued relentlessly by a horde of beasts, but thankfully the fort proved able to repel their assault. Given that we were not in much shape to continue fighting, and as the barracks were relatively secure, we made use of the beds there for a

Page 4

much-needed rest. After what must have been a few hours, we were roused from our slumber by a crash. It came from the direction of the door; a battering ram! Our pursuers must've grabbed one after all. Still, the door held,

Page 5

but it wouldn't for long. Fighting within the cramped confines of the barracks was an unappealing prospect, and we had no way of ascertaining the number of our attackers. As we were, we were totally backed into a corner.

Page 6

However, there was one possibility. In one of the corners was a ladder leading up to a small observational tower. Though we could've all slipped out that way, we would've needed to leave the Eye behind, an unacceptable prospect after all that

Page 7

had happened. Instead, I directed three of the other men to escape via that route to ambush our attackers, whilst Frederick and I remained to safeguard the Eye, and to dispatch any foes that might break through our defenses.

Page 8

Up the ladder they went. Bang! The door began to buckle. The men were now gone. A pause; Bang! The wood of the door was audibly splintering, and was quite dented; it wouldn't take

Page 9

another hit. We held our swords at the ready. Another dreadful pause; Bang! The door shattered into pieces, and this was accompanied by a pained, beastly yowling outside.

Page 10

Taking advantage of the confusion brewing outside, we rushed out to assist. We faced off against a score of adversaries, slaying many in the process. A few of them fled, scurrying off into the darkness. It was just as well; we'd need all our

Page 11

strength for the long trek ahead of us. It took a day and a half for us to trace our way back through the caverns safely. Beasts set themselves upon us in varying frequencies, though more often than not they seemed

Page 12

to suffer from some sort of frightfully frenzied state, as opposed to the simple bloodlust that they'd previously greeted us with on our descent. Might it be that the Eye has some strange effect upon them, or are they just simply in

Page 13

disarray with the death of their monstrous leader? It is impossible to say at this point, but I can't help but feel a tinge of worry over it. As we passed through the burned-out spider caves, we had a

Page 14

most unusual encounter. We saw our torchlight reflecting off the eyes of something nearby, and readied for another fight. We drew near, but were initially relieved to find that it was, of all things, Robert! He had,

Page 15

apparently, somehow survived the water-dwelling horrors of the limestone caverns. Numerous questions sprang into my mind, but before I could speak any of them, something about Robert caught my

Page 16

attention: I surely would've expected him to look rather haggard after such an ordeal, but there was more to it than that alone. His skin was visibly slick, of a sickly pallor, and it looked as though scales were beginning to

Page 17

form in some patches; his eyes were more bulbous than I'd remembered them being, and he seemed to look through us entirely; and on the sides of his neck were several slits, which had an appearance vaguely like the

Page 18

gills of a fish. What horrors had our comrade gone through? It was only a scant few moments after making these observations that our presence seemed to register with Robert, and upon doing

Page 19

so, he violently threw himself at Leonard, thrashing at him all the while. As it seemed our old friend was no more, having gone totally mad, I was about to strike at him when Leonard managed to beat him away with a couple firm punches. Robert, or

Page 20

whatever faint shadow of him remained, scrambled away and ran off into the darkness. I'm not sure if I should be relieved that we safely got through this encounter, or if I ought despair that this is how we'd last remember him.

Page 21

We found our way back to the encampment at long last, and with sufficient time to spare. Wilhelm's absence from our party was apparent to the other men, and we heard ill news from them regarding Gerald, who had finally succumbed to

Page 22

the spider's venom some hours after we'd initially left. Robert, Kenneth, Wilhelm, and now Gerald. The danger of this undertaking was understood by all, but that does not make it any less painful to suffer the consequences.

Page 23

Will your sacrifices have been worth it in the end? For now, we can only hope. The hour of our escape draws near, and the barrage against our defenses is intensifying. If that strange mystic, the one guarding the seal,

Page 24

doesn't hold true to his word, then we will all surely meet our end down in these depths. It's nearly time. We must prepare.
