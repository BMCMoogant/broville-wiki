Museums and memory

Broville's cultural life includes museums, cinema, sports, journalism and entertainment. The city is home to the Museum of Science and Cine Slant Movie Studios, as well as the Museum of Natural History, presented by the community description as one of the world's most visited museums.

The Museum of Natural History is particularly known for its complete T-Rex bone collection. Community history dates its construction to 1867, as part of Broville's centenary. The museum also displays objects from ancient peoples and, underground, a technology that calls the schoolbook account of the war into question.

Broville Memorial Pantheon forms part of the city's geography of monuments and public ceremonies.

Journalism and leisure

Edward Page searches the Broville Beat for old news to understand the station that became his bookstore. The newspaper thus also serves as a record of urban memory.

The casinos, Gamebros arcade and stadium show everyday activities beyond the laboratories. The movie studios add filmmaking to this picture.
