Bembe Coto

CharactersEdit articleSuggest a changeSources

A figure remembered as the monastery’s founder. His testament describes what the mission did to the former inhabitants.

The testament

Bembe describes the religious mission’s violence against the inhabitants of Brolapagos. His text mentions a key and a clue connected with water. The book is in the monastery’s burial area and leads into the investigation of the mission.

His memorial and final request

Two inscriptions in the monastery identify Bembe as its founding father and mark his ashes. My Final presents itself as his last will and testament, rather than an account written by a later visitor.

Bembe describes the destruction of the island’s earlier culture as his one regret. His confession and the request he leaves behind are central to The Monastery’s Secret.

Spoiler warningReveal spoiler

Bembe says that the original inhabitants were killed during the mission’s attempt to convert them. Their bodies and cultural remains were buried in pits beneath the mission. He claims to have preserved something that the other monks would plunder if they discovered it.

His instructions ask the reader to bring back a key so that he can illuminate the way. The warning about what lies below is part of Bembe’s own account of the people his mission destroyed.
