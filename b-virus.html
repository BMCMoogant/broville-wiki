The samples

The notebook Inorganic Sentience describes matter that heats up, begins moving, or changes shape. One sample survives freezing, division, and thawing.

What the notebook actually tests

Inorganic Sentience records B-Virus being administered to two different materials in July 1998. The magma-cream variant produces rapid heat and movement; a second mixture, made from moss and pig brain tissue, takes a cubic shape and hops.

The second material grows overnight and remains active after freezing, division and thawing. The notebook describes these observed results and the researchers’ containment measures; it does not identify the virus’s origin.
