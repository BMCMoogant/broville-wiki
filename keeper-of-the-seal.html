The expedition’s passage

The Keeper of the Seal is the elder who opens the passage for Heraldus’s expedition. As he lets the group enter, he promises to reopen the way after ten days.

Page 1

Preface Written in this book shall be a brief account of my and my men's journey here, deep beneath the earth. As for how we came to this place, I shall explain. An unfamiliar and evidently ancient tome was recently

Page 2

discovered in our library by one of our scribes. Whether the thing had simply been forgotten about, or if it had somehow materialized on its own, none can as of yet say. In any event, the book makes reference to a world very near to ours which is

Page 3

ordinarily imperceptible. Included in this tome were descriptions of strange locales, and even stranger inhabitants of these places. Of particular note was the mentioning of a peculiar artifact that lies in the possession of some ruler of this realm.

Page 4

The artifact may supposedly endow its owner with a variety of effects, such as unnatural strength, endless vigor, the reversal of aging, the materialization of various foodstuffs, and other such effects.

Page 5

Whether or not such a thing truly exists, one cannot say, but if these tall tales are true then I would make this artifact my own, and with the assistance of our kingdom's greatest minds, I would bring prosperity to my kingdom.

Page 6

It is for this purpose that we come to this place. We shall seek out its owner and request the artifact. If they will not see reason, then we will have to pry it loose from their lifeless hands.

Page 7

More recently, a watchtower guard reported a most bizarre sighting. During midday, he claimed that off in the distance the air began to shimmer and ripple, as though caused by a great heat. Within this rippling air began to collect a kind of "shadow-

Page 8

stuff" as he put it, condensing in greater and greater quantities until it seemed to totally solidify into some kind of stony structure. By his account, the rippling ceased afterward. When he reported this story, he was first accused of

Page 9

drunkenness, but a cursory investigation revealed that there was in fact a kind of stone hut now located out in the swamp, at the location he indicated.

Page 10

Regarding the aforementioned tome, the stone structure identically matched an illustration found in it. It is clear that the way has been opened to us, and we shall make use of this opportunity while we still have it.

Page 11

Day 1 After gathering around myself a group of my bravest and most skilled knights, we collected some supplies together and set off for the mysterious stone structure.

Page 12

We crossed the marsh and approached the structure. On the north face we found an entryway; within was a staircase leading down into the earth. At the bottom of the flight of stairs, we came to a doorway with a warning engraved above it:

Page 13

"Abandon all hope, ye who enter here", it read. Undeterred by this message, we pressed onward. Beyond this doorway, we passed through a series of rooms and narrow tunnels. Some of these tunnels were rigged with traps of various designs,

Page 14

though after their initial discovery we managed to continue rather unscathed. At last we arrived at an unusual chamber. Towards the center of it rested a flat, circular, white slab that appeared to rest on top of a stone base. Occupying the

Page 15

chamber was a man of very advanced age. Wondering what such a man would be doing in such a place, we asked him about this matter. He replied, stating that he was the "keeper of the seal", and that he had been awaiting our arrival. Without further

Page 16

prompting, the old man arose from his seated position and shuffled over to the circular slab. Evoking a strength that could not have possibly matched his frail-looking body, the "keeper", as he called himself, pulled the slab away, revealing a

Page 17

pit of inky blackness far below. He informed us that he would have to replace the slab after we passed through, and that he would pull it away again only once more in ten day's time. Evidently our mission would be a timed one.

Page 18

We cast down a rope ladder and began our descent. After Wilhelm, the last member of our party, cleared the gap, the slab above was pushed back into place, and what little light streamed in from above was cut off.

Page 19

Frederick opted to shed some light on our surroundings by lighting a torch. We found ourselves in a rather ordinary-looking cave, although we could scarcely see more than a few feet without the aid of a light source.

Page 20

We agreed that setting up an encampment would be our first priority, so we set about searching for a suitable location. We soon found a defensible position that had sufficient space for our needs. Using the materials we brought along with us,

Page 21

we set up two makeshift defensive walls to block off our camp area from the surrounding caves. Afterwards, we set about pitching our tents, lighting the fire, etc. This area will serve us suitably during our time down here.

Page 22

In the morning, we shall split up into three groups so as to cover more ground in our scouting. By day's end, we will all return to the encampment to report on our findings.
